TAC Scorpion revival

Jun 2022Past Projects1 min readBy Thei Munten

On the bench is a TAC Scorpion console from the late '80s, heavy as an armoured tank and missing its VU meters. The project is part repair, part investigation into why I preferred it over the Soundcrafts.

Back in the days when everybody was using some Soundcraft n*200 (n = 1, 2, 3, 4...) mixing desk, I used to prefer a TAC Scorpion console. We are talking end of the '80s, beginning of the '90s here. So this is my way-too-much-work project to investigate why I preferred that console over others, and at the same time make it a bit more 2022.

Now, this is most certainly not how these consoles looked, so let's start with a picture of an original TAC Scorpion. This is not my original console; that one has disappeared into oblivion. But it is very similar to the one shown, and all VU meters were broken. They all had that after a year or two.

So instead of trying to fix those, the first thing I did was get rid of some heavy metal casing. Boy, are these things built like heavily armoured tanks.

I do like VU meters on every channel, though. Not so much to adjust gain (oh man, I can rant about that one), but they come in handy to quickly see which synth, sequencer or vocal channel is doing that solo in a live situation. 😏

So I made a VU board with a vintage chip everybody knows: the LM3915. As there is no audio passing through this chip, I didn't care where it came from: AliExpress FTW! I designed a simple PCB in cool black, and here you go: a DIY VU meter. I mounted them to the faders, which I had to clean anyway. I used the same feed point for the fader and the measuring input (as in PFL), so no long lines running across the console.
